What we learned

01

The cascade beats either half

The on-box detector is quiet (0.7% of normal videos alarmed, 0.17–0.33 alarms per hour) but misses exact moments; the VLM names crimes but is slow and, in the cloud, costly. Screening first, naming second, keeps both strengths.

02

Fine-tuning needs labels, not just GPUs

On 11 hand-labelled theft videos, fine-tuned VideoMAE did not beat frozen features (frame AUROC 0.852 frozen vs 0.78–0.80, paired bootstrap, 3 seeds), though LoRA lifted recall at 1 false alarm an hour from 3% to 19%.

03

Local is private and cheap; cloud is sharper

Gemini names 64% of crimes; Sentinel Machines v1, running on the box, names 57% with no footage leaving the store. Our roadmap closes the gap with more labelled incidents, not bigger models.

What sets us apart

01

Footage never leaves the building

Every frame is analysed on the HP ZGX Nano (NVIDIA GB10) inside the store, not in a third-party data centre. CCTV of customers and staff is some of the most sensitive data a shop holds, and with us it stays on the premises. Recordings and review decisions are stored locally too, so there's no central server holding it all.

02

Cloud-level accuracy, without the cloud bill

Cloud AI charges for every frame it sees, so the bill grows with every camera and every hour. On our edge box, extra analysis costs nothing. We haven't given up quality for that: on the same 36-clip UCF-Crime test, the model on the GB10 scored 69 and the same model through the cloud scored 64. Both caught 83% of crimes with the same 22% false-alarm rate. The Analytics page shows the savings live, with "Saved vs API" and cost per camera-hour side by side.

03

Faster than real time, with or without internet

The GB10 analyses each 8-second window of video in a median 2.1 seconds, about 4× faster than the footage plays (163 windows in 146 s). Because it runs on site, there are no network outages, API rate limits or provider downtime. That matters when a detection may need to trigger a 911 call.

04

An AI analyst on watch, not a flood of alerts

Other systems send a ping for every detection until staff stop reading them. Our Hermes agent runs on the edge box and reviews every feed every 5 minutes. It merges repeated detections, drops low-confidence and already-dismissed events, and posts a briefing you can read in 20 seconds: what's happening, what needs attention now, and what's worth knowing. Every hour it adds a digest of patterns, such as the same crime repeating at one store or a camera going silent. Every claim has to cite a real incident ID, and the server rejects anything it can't verify. The agent can't send alerts or change reviews, so a human always makes the final call.
