01 · Problem

Recording is not watching

A shop already has the cameras. What it does not have is anyone with the attention to watch forty feeds at once — so footage is something you scroll through after the loss, if at all.

01

Nobody is watching live

One person cannot hold forty feeds in their head. Attention runs out long before the shift does.

02

Footage is read backwards

Video gets opened after the stock count, when the person is long gone and the clip is one of a thousand hours.

03

Motion alerts cry wolf

A pixel-difference alarm fires on a shadow, a trolley, a door. Staff learn to ignore it within a week.

04

Cloud video is a liability

Shipping a shop floor to someone else's datacentre is a privacy problem, a bandwidth bill and a dependency.

02 · Solution

Built to watch, not to judge

It names what happened and hands it to a person. Three lines it does not cross.

01

It describes, never identifies

No identity database and no watchlist. It reports what people do, never who they are.

02

Suspected, not accused

Every detection waits for a human. The system never accuses anyone, and alerts are opt-in and capped.

03

Footage stays in the building

With the local model, frames only ever reach the GB10 on site. Cloud models are opt-in, for comparison.
