        The stock count finds a phone missing.

        Someone scrubs 40 hours of footage looking for it, if anyone bothers at all.

      The camera saw everything. Nobody was watching.

      Solution

      Sentinel Machines turns the cameras you already have into a security team that never looks away.

      01

      Every camera, watched around the clock

      It looks at every camera every eight seconds, day and night, and never gets tired or distracted. No new cameras and no extra hires: it works with the footage your cameras already record.

      02

      Know what happened, not just that something moved

      Instead of another motion beep, your team gets a plain-English alert: what happened, who to look at and how sure the AI is. It arrives in about two seconds, with the video cued to the exact moment.

      03

      Private by design, fair by default

      Footage never leaves the store, and there's no cloud bill that grows with every camera. It describes actions, never identities, and flags incidents as suspected, so a person always makes the final call.

      What we learned

      01

      Benchmark the deployment, not the model name

      The same weights gave different answers depending on where they ran. Qwen3-VL-8B false-alarmed 9.5% of normal clips through a cloud provider and 4.1% on the box; Qwen3.8-27B named 28.7% vs 24.0% and took 9.49 s vs 2.84 s. Provider, quantisation and serving stack all move the numbers, so every candidate is scored through our own pipeline on our own clips.

      02

      Our biggest model got more cautious, not more accurate

      Qwen3.8-27B named the fewest crimes (24–29%) but almost never raised a false alarm (0–1.4%); Qwen3-VL-8B named more (37–40%) and alarmed more (4–10%). Picking a model means picking an operating point: the screener should catch everything, the model that pages a guard should rarely be wrong. That split is why the system is a cascade.

      03

      Models spot theft videos, not the moment of theft

      Frozen video features separate theft footage from normal footage (frame AUROC 0.852), but inside a theft video none of the seven detector setups we tested, frozen or fine-tuned, could tell the seconds of concealment from the rest (AUROC 0.48–0.58, where 0.5 is chance). Fine-tuning on 11 hand-labelled videos didn't fix it (0.78–0.80). So a person reviews the whole window, and the next investment is hand-marked theft times, not bigger GPUs.

      04

      Batching is what makes one box enough

      Qwen3-VL-8B takes 2.93 s per window one at a time but 0.64 s per window batched. At one window every 8 seconds per camera, that is about 12 cameras in real time on one model instance, where batch 1 covers under 3. Throughput per box, not latency per call, decides how many stores the edge can serve.

      What sets us apart

      01

      Footage never leaves the building

      Every frame is analysed on the HP ZGX Nano (NVIDIA GB10) inside the store, not in a third-party data centre. CCTV of customers and staff is some of the most sensitive data a shop holds, and with us it stays on the premises. Recordings and review decisions are stored locally too, so there's no central server holding it all.

      02

      Cloud-level accuracy, without the cloud bill

      Cloud AI charges for every frame it sees, so the bill grows with every camera and every hour. On our edge box, extra analysis costs nothing. We haven't given up quality for that: on the same 36-clip UCF-Crime test, the model on the GB10 scored 69 and the same model through the cloud scored 64. Both caught 83% of crimes with the same 22% false-alarm rate. The Analytics page shows the savings live, with "Saved vs API" and cost per camera-hour side by side.

      03

      Faster than real time, with or without internet

      The GB10 analyses each 8-second window of video in a median 2.1 seconds, about 4× faster than the footage plays (163 windows in 146 s). Because it runs on site, there are no network outages, API rate limits or provider downtime. That matters when a detection may need to trigger a 911 call.

      04

      An AI analyst on watch, not a flood of alerts

      Other systems send a ping for every detection until staff stop reading them. Our Hermes agent runs on the edge box and reviews every feed every 5 minutes. It merges repeated detections, drops low-confidence and already-dismissed events, and posts a briefing you can read in 20 seconds: what's happening, what needs attention now, and what's worth knowing. Every hour it adds a digest of patterns, such as the same crime repeating at one store or a camera going silent. Every claim has to cite a real incident ID, and the server rejects anything it can't verify. The agent can't send alerts or change reviews, so a human always makes the final call.
